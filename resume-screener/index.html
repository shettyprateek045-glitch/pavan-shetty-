Resume Screening Helper

Rank fictional candidates against a job description. Explorer uses skills and keywords. Innovator turns the same text into embeddings, explains every rank, and checks whether the shortlist treats groups evenly.

How to do this

  1. Split the record. Score only skills and experience. Keep name, pronouns, region, and school in a side record the ranker does not read.
  2. Explorer. Pull a skill list out of the job description. Score each resume by how many of those skills it has, plus the share of meaningful words it shares with the description. Sort by that score.
  3. Innovator embeddings. Treat each document as a vector of word weights (TF-IDF). Rare words that appear in both the job and the resume count more than words that appear everywhere. Relevance is the cosine of the two vectors. Swap this function later for a neural model if you want meaning beyond shared words.
  4. Explain the rank. List matched skills, missing skills, and the shared terms that contributed most to the cosine. A number without those terms is not an explanation.
  5. Fairness check, after scoring. Take the top of the list. For each group, selection rate is how many from that group were shortlisted divided by how many were in the pool. Disparate impact is the lowest rate divided by the highest. Under 0.8 is the four-fifths rule of thumb: review the gap. It is a diagnostic, not a legal ruling.
  6. Prove the check can fail. A “school prestige” boost ignores skills and favors one region’s schools. Blind mode should stay near even. The boost should trip the region check while pronouns stay even, which shows the audit is looking at outcomes, not at a single story.
